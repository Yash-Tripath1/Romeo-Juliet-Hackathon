1. Abstract and thesis
We reproduce the incumbent's 0.525 versus 0.350 starter greedy on ten public seeds, then measure how
much room is left. Under complete disclosure the reciprocal hard-constraint graph has mean degree 2.53
and 33.9% of members with no feasible partner; the fraction ever introducible is 0.399 (development),
0.341 (cold start), 0.178 (sparse). Every asking rule reaches 97 to 98% of that ceiling, so the primary
score is supply-determined: asking is a step function (+0.183, CI [+0.042, +0.325]), while targeting,
weighting and adaptation differences run −0.000 to +0.117 at a fixed matcher, none conclusive. The pre-
specified incumbent versus greedy contrast is +0.175, CI [+0.092, +0.258], p = 0.016. An oracle with per‐
fect clarification adds 0.001 coverage and 1.2 assignments: information is not the binding constraint.
At a glance
Scale: 1,326 valid episodes (1,020 head-to-head, 180 oracle, 108 logging, 18 equivalence); zero invalid
actions.
Structure: coverage ceilings 0.399 / 0.341 / 0.178 and effective-edge caps 118.0 / 90.6 / 23.3; every
asking rule at 97 to 98% of ceiling.
What moves: asking vs none +0.183 [+0.042, +0.325]; incumbent vs greedy (pre-specified) +0.175
[+0.092, +0.258], p = 0.016; weight coding plus global matching +0.133 [+0.008, +0.258].
What does not move: ask targeting −0.000 to +0.117 (all ns); fitted prior vs hand-coded weights
−0.125 [−0.192, −0.067]; online adaptation 0 wins / 60 ties / 0 losses; residual soft asks +0.017 (ns).
Bound: best screened cell 0.592 is best of 17 and needs a fresh-seed retest; the attainable primary band
is about 0.5 to 0.8; a perfect-information oracle adds 0.001 coverage.
Thesis. With reciprocal hard feasibility enforced, MSMI is determined first by whether a policy makes the feas‐
ible graph usable at all, a step function that any clarification rule, however naive, saturates; second by how
much of the resulting edge supply it consumes before members exit or retire, which we measure at 68 to 87%
and which no rule we tested moved; and only third, within a band narrower than 60 episodes can resolve, by 
how well it ranks the pairs it gets to choose between.

2.2 Three-valued reciprocal feasibility
The supplied eligibility(a, b) returns one of three values, and the distinction is the hinge of the whole
problem:
infeasible: at least one observed hard check fails in either direction. No clarification rescues this pair.
needs clarification: no observed check fails, but at least one hard field is 
. The pair is blocked; it
may be feasible or infeasible and the policy cannot know which.
feasible: every hard field observed for both members and every reciprocal check passes. Only these
pairs may be proposed.
Reciprocity means each check runs twice, once with each member as constraint holder: 's age must lie in

2.4 What the policy may observe
Only observe() output: arrived members with values, statuses and observation days; the introduction list;
feedback whose observed_day has been reached; the cumulative ask log; the day; remaining budget. No lat‐

receives the public episode seed as a deterministic offset for its own tie-break shuffles (§5.1); the seed is
used only to make reruns reproducible, never as a lookup into or inference about outcomes, and no latent
or private data crosses the interface. We use exactly this, plus offline-fitted assets derived from our own
rollouts on declared training seeds.

4.3 Modelling: a restricted pair-outcome prior
A randomised logging policy on eighteen declared training seeds (disjoint from the ten evaluation seeds)
produced 108 episodes and 7,613 labelled introductions, 4,516 with both members responding; fea‐
tures are recorded at assignment, labels harvested only after the 40-day follow-up. The target is
 over the 7-day window: a direct MSMI model at a 1.13% base
rate achieves held-out AUC 0.581, essentially no discrimination, while the acceptance stage carries the
same pair-level signal at 40× the label density. Three nested ridge-logistic specifications were compared
on six held-out training seeds; the shipped four-feature specification B wins on every held-out measure
(log loss 0.5513, Brier 0.1830, AUC 0.598), beating both the 7-indicator spec A and the 18-feature spec C,
which loses despite four times the parameters because observability is a confound, not a quality sig‐
nal: mutual acceptance is 0.2724 with no soft field known and 0.2317 with all seven known. The shipped
coefficients, the full specification search and the held-out calibration table are in Appendix B. The
shipped model is calibrated out of sample (spec B: held-out log loss 0.5513, Brier 0.1830 on 1,462
pairs from six held-out seeds, central bins within 0.040 of nominal), and §6.2c shows that calibration is not
what makes a good edge weight here. Directional probabilities are not multiplied: the observed joint
rate is 0.2538 against a product of fitted directionals of 0.2297 (ratio 1.105), so we model the joint directly.
4.4 Residual-budget soft clarification
The 12-unit daily budget is not binding: measured spend is ≈195 units per episode against a 720 ceiling
because 31.9% of development members are decline-blocked, 36.4% arrive complete, and the askable pop‐
ulation is exhausted well before the horizon (§6.6). Outcome-relevant soft fields are observed for only
~46% of members in development and ~34% in cold start, and no baseline spends a unit on them. Once
hard-bundle asks are exhausted, our rule converts residual units into 1-unit soft answers, prioritising
(member, field) pairs by 
, lifting realised spend from 195 to ~558 units. Whether that
converts into MSMI is measured in §6, not assumed.
4.5 Online adaptation on dense proxies
MSMI matures 30+ days after assignment, about one positive label per episode, so an MSMI-only bandit is
structurally starved. Directional responses (two per introduction, visible within seven days) give ~120 us‐
able observations by mid-episode; we refit the §4.3 model online by ridge-anchored IRLS towards the off‐
line prior, excluding non-responses from the update, and admit a single day/60 coefficient, zero in the off‐
line prior, for drift/shift, whose measured acceptance falls 0.263 → 0.237 → 0.164 across day blocks. Car‐
ried memory (coefficient vector, bounded feature buffer, counters) stays within the 1 MiB cap; we will veri‐
fy the serialised size before Round 2.
4.6 Audit ledger and deliberate non-actions
Every proposed pair emits the observed evidence per field, unresolved fields, the binding constraint when
rejected, the runner-up and its weight, and the reduced profit; explanations keep model uncertainty, 
missing data and stochastic outcome separate, and no latent value is ever presented as an observation.
We deliberately did not: read hidden state at inference; build a latent-probability oracle rung (the simulat‐
or exposes no latent pair probabilities, so it was dropped); claim inverse-propensity results on the supplied
files (propensity is null; doubly-robust evaluation on our own logs is planned); or describe a Thompson-
plus-value-of-information hybrid we do not implement.

Training and evaluation seeds are disjoint; the split is by world, the only unit that respects the dependence
structure, since all 200 members of a pool share one generator draw. Every method sees the identical
(seed, variant) worlds and policy memory is empty at every episode start. Randomness is fully de‐
clared. The world generator is seeded by the simulator from (seed, variant) and we never touch it. Inside
the policy, every draw uses a fixed generator seeded only from public inputs: the shuffled feasible matcher
uses random.Random(17 + day) and the residual-soft ask tiebreak uses random.Random(917 + seed), where
seed is the public episode identifier handed to the policy for reproducibility; it enters only as an offset into
our own shuffle, is never used to infer an outcome, and the same offsets are used for every configuration
so that ties in §6 are ties of rule rather than of draw. There is no wall-clock, no os.urandom and no unseeded
random call in the decision path, so a rerun is bit-identical. Statistical resampling uses ran‐
dom.Random(20261009), fixed independently of the policy. All results are in-process against the public simu‐
lator on public seeds, not official subprocess or Docker timings.

7. Failure analysis
7.1 Sparse geography: supply, not policy
Twelve opaque zones with single-zone acceptance put 67.0% of sparse members at degree zero and leave
23.3 usable pairs; consuming every one of them at the measured funnel yields 0.122 MSMI per 100
against 0.619 for a development world at full utilisation, so sparse is a floor on the primary score, not a
test of skill. Combining the edge-supply caps with the funnel gives an arithmetic bound on the primary
score at different pair-selection qualities (Appendix B): 0.512 at our measured logging quality, 0.606 at the
incumbent's published quality, up to 1.009 at best-case selection. This is a bound, not a forecast: it as‐
sumes every effective edge is consumed and ignores timing. Our randomised logging policy consumed 71%
of the edge supply in development (87% in sparse) and scored 0.375 against the 0.512 bound, so the hon‐
est reading is that the achievable primary sits between roughly 0.5 and 0.8, and both the published in‐
cumbent (0.525) and our best screened cell (0.592) are inside that band. Any claim of a large multiple over
the incumbent should be treated with suspicion until the bound is beaten, not merely approached. The cor‐
rect sparse strategy is selective, not expansive: with 23 pairs, revealing the good ones early beats reveal‐
ing all of them, because members exit. Caveat: only three distinct sparse worlds exist among the ten public
seeds.
7.2 Withheld answers: a permanently excluded third of the pool
Because declined is absorbing, 31.9% of development and 41.0% of cold-start members can never appear
in a feasible pair; unioned with degree-zero isolation the never-introducible share is 60.2% / 66.0% / 82.3%
across development / cold start / sparse (Figure 8, Appendix B). Three consequences: coverage must be re‐
ported against the ceiling, not against 1.0 (our best configuration reaches 0.465 on development seed 101,
equal to that world's per-seed ceiling); clarification spent on decline-blocked members is destroyed, and
every ask rule we test filters them first; and missingness here is informative about availability, not prefer‐
ence (acceptance is lower when more soft fields are observed, 0.2724 to 0.2317), so any model encoding
observability as a feature learns a spurious preference; the fix is to exclude the indicator, not re-weight by
it.

7.6 Competition for the same candidate
At mean degree 2.53 competition is the norm; a max-weight matching resolves it globally while the starter
greedy resolves it by sort order. Two dynamic forms remain named, not solved: temporal competition
(introducing  to  today consumes the pair permanently and makes both busy for eight days; if  has a bet‐
ter partner currently busy or not yet arrived, waiting dominates; H4) and success consumes supply (a
mutual second-Yes retires both members, so maximising MSMI spends the scarcest resource in the system,
and the optimal policy may be more selective than the single-day objective implies; quantifying this needs
a two-stage look-ahead we have not implemented).

8.4 Synthetic-data disclaimer
All members, profiles, preferences, conversations, introductions and outcomes are newly generated or au‐
thored synthetic content; demographic proportions and outcome behaviour are invented assumptions, not
fitted distributions of real people. Simulator performance is an engineering and research test, not evidence
that any model can predict a relationship, and nothing here should be presented as product effectiveness.
Specifically, these invented outcomes cannot establish that any feature we weighted predicts real compat‐
ibility (we recovered the generator's decision, not the world's); that a higher MSMI is a better product out‐
come (MSMI is a synthetic proxy whose funnel is dominated by invented response and punctuality rates);
that clarification is acceptable to real people (a simulated member never experiences being asked, declin‐
ing, or objecting to timing); or anything about fairness (the generator draws acceptance uniformly and in‐
dependently of demographics, so our runs contain no bias to detect, and absence of measured disparity
here is not evidence of absence in production). Real use would require, in order: a schema adapter with an
audited missing-answer rule; a consent layer with per-question withdrawal, since declined must be re‐
spectful in production as it is absorbing here; verified-attribute eligibility with a human review path; out‐
come labels from actual consented follow-up, which would face the censoring problem of §2.3; prospective
validation powered on the event rate that actually occurs; and a fairness audit across protected attributes,
which this simulator structurally cannot supply. None of that is started. The method is portable (three-val‐
ued feasibility algebra, supply diagnosis, matching formulation, seed-block statistics), but every number in
§6 is a property of a generator we did not write.
